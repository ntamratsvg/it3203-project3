Evolution of the Cloud

The roots of cloud computing date back to the 1960s with the concept of "time-sharing" on giant mainframe computers. Before the internet was common, multiple users would access a single powerful computer from different terminals, which laid the groundwork for modern resource sharing. During this era, researchers envisioned a global network where computing power could be accessed as a utility, much like water or electricity.

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, the concept evolved into "grid computing" and eventually the modern cloud. Companies like Salesforce led the way by delivering applications over the internet, while Amazon Web Services revolutionized the industry by renting out excess server capacity to other businesses. This shifted the burden of maintaining expensive physical hardware away from small businesses and onto specialized providers, allowing for the rapid growth of the digital economy.

The Three Service Models

To understand the modern cloud, we categorize services based on how much control the user has over the infrastructure:

  • IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service): This is the most flexible model where you rent the raw hardware, such as virtual servers and storage. You are responsible for managing the operating system, security, and applications.
  • PaaS (Platform as a Service): This model is designed for developers. The provider manages the servers and operating systems, allowing you to focus entirely on writing and deploying your code without worrying about the back-end.
  • SaaS (Software as a Service): This is the most common model for everyday users. You access finished software applications through a web browser, such as Microsoft 365, Google Drive, or Netflix, with the provider handling all technical aspects.
